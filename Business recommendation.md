# **Business recommendations – Vendor Performance Analysis**

# 

### 1\. Optimize vendor selection

Finding: analysis compares vendor spending and average delivery duration.



Recommendation:

\- Identify vendors offering competitive purchase prices and shorter delivery times.

\- Prioritize reliable vendors for regular procurement.

\- Negotiate better prices with high-spending suppliers.

\- Avoid depending entirely on a single supplier.

Expected business benefit: Reduced procurement costs and improved supply reliability.







### 2\. Reduce procurement costs

Finding: analyzed purchase spending by product classification and vendor.



Recommendation:

\- Identify product classifications consuming the largest purchasing budget.

\- Review high-value purchases regularly.

\- Negotiate bulk purchase discounts where demand justifies them.

\- Compare supplier prices for identical products before placing orders.

Expected business benefit: Better utilization of the procurement budget.





### 3\. Improve inventory management

Finding: overall inventory increased from 4,219,275 units to 4,885,776 units, an increase of approximately 15.79%.



Recommendation:

\- Investigate why inventory increased.

\- Identify products with unusually high closing stock.

\- Review purchasing frequency for products with high stock and low purchase activity.

\- Establish appropriate minimum and maximum stock levels.

Expected business benefit: Reduced inventory holding costs and less capital tied up in stock.





### 4\. Investigate zero-stock products

Finding: ending inventory contains products with zero stock.



Recommendation:

\- Identify frequently zero-stock products across stores.

\- Investigate whether these products experience high demand.

\- Improve replenishment planning for important products.

\- Maintain safety stock for products with consistently high demand.

Expected business benefit: Lower risk of lost sales due to unavailable products.



Note: Zero stock alone does not prove a stockout; sales and demand data would help confirm this.





### 5\. Improve supplier delivery performance

Finding: calculated average delivery duration using PODate and ReceivingDate.



Recommendation:

\- Monitor average delivery lead time by vendor.

\- Investigate vendors with consistently longer delivery durations.

\- Discuss delivery schedules and lead-time expectations with suppliers.

\- Maintain alternative suppliers for critical products.

Expected business benefit: More predictable procurement and reduced supply disruption risk.





### 6\. Control freight expenses

Finding: freight analysis identified unusual freight amounts and some high freight percentages.



Recommendation:

\- Investigate suppliers with unusually high freight-to-purchase-value ratios.

\- Negotiate freight terms with frequently used vendors.

\- Consolidate orders where practical to reduce transportation costs.

\- Monitor freight expenses as a percentage of total purchases.

Expected business benefit: Lower transportation and procurement expenses.





### 7\. Improve product profitability

Finding: calculated estimated gross margin percentages using selling price and purchase price.



Recommendation:

\- Identify products with low estimated gross margins.

\- Review supplier costs for low-margin products.

\- Negotiate better purchasing prices.

\- Evaluate whether high-margin products have sufficient sales volume before prioritizing them.

Expected business benefit: Improved product-level gross margins.



Note: Your margin calculation excludes freight, operating expenses, discounts and taxes.





### 8\. Reduce supplier dependency

Finding: analyzed the number of unique vendors and vendor spending contributions.



Recommendation:

\- Identify vendors contributing a large percentage of total purchasing expenditure.

\- Evaluate the risk of relying heavily on a small number of suppliers.

\- Develop alternative supplier relationships for critical products.

\- Review supplier concentration periodically.

Expected business benefit: Reduced supply-chain risk and better negotiating flexibility.





### 9\. Improve procurement planning using historical trends

Finding: project includes purchase dates, quantities and spending information.



Recommendation:

\- Monitor monthly purchase spending and quantity trends.

\- Identify periods with unusually high purchasing activity.

\- Use historical purchase patterns to plan procurement budgets.

\- Coordinate purchasing decisions with inventory requirements.

Expected business benefit: Better purchasing decisions and more efficient cash-flow planning.
